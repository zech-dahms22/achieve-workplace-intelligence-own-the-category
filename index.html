02 The Framework

Build

You show up where the right buyers gather and earn a relationship in the room. Trust starts accruing.

  • Curated dinners and roundtables

Each touch makes the next one cheaper.

Own

A relationship and reputation with your buyers that persists between campaigns. Your ICP expects to see you.

  • Series based partnerships
  • A named point of view the market repeats
  • Advocates who return
Pipeline that shows up before you spend.
Rent buys reach. Build buys trust. Own buys a head start on every future quarter.
03 The Rule of Seven

Nobody buys on the first touch.

There is a number every seasoned marketer keeps coming back to. It takes roughly seven touches before a buyer acts. Not one. Seven. It is the oldest rule in the discipline, and it is the one that decides whether a program builds pipeline or just builds activity.

Touches 1-2

Stranger

They half-notice you. Nothing is earned yet. This is the most expensive touch you will ever buy, because you are reaching someone cold.

Touches 3-5

Familiar, then trusted

Your point of view starts to register, seen more than once, in more than one place. Recognition turns into credibility.

Touch 7+

Ready to act

Now the ask lands, because it arrives on top of six touches that made it feel obvious instead of cold.

This is the mechanism underneath Rent, Build, and Own. A one-off engagement buys a single touch and then stops, which is why it evaporates. Category ownership is what happens when the right buyers see you land the same point of view again and again until buying is the natural next step. It is also why we build in campaigns, not one-offs: the first touch is the priciest, every touch after it gets cheaper, and quitting after one means you paid full freight for the hardest part and walked away from the payoff.

A single webcast asks a stranger to buy on the first hello. A campaign earns the seventh touch, where the buying actually happens.
